What the shape says

  • The concentration happened. The last seven days carried 37% of the flight's spend, which is the back-load we set out to run.
  • Registrations did not follow at the same rate. Those seven days produced 26% of registrations, so cost per registration in the final window was $72 against $40 earlier, about 78% higher.
  • That is the trade, stated plainly. Late registrations cost more to buy. They are also the ones that attend at roughly twice the rate of the mid-flight window, so the test is whether the better show rate outweighs the higher acquisition cost. Atlanta's 15.35% overall show rate is the first data point; Reno will be the cleaner read.
